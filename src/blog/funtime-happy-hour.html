homelabbing (how to tempt financial ruin)

as an example of a project i've been able to take up or more realistically got back on the bandwagon with, i woke up one morning and glanced across my room to the rockpi thats been sitting on my silicon mat on the 'tech and tools' section of my utility shelf. it was what i used to set up my media server almost a year ago now. and it worked! for about 4 months! then it died! i took one second to examine this thing that i'd been dreading going back into; the setup was an absolute slog, and thought

"yeah, i could do that today".

2 days later it was back online. the generic sd card i used for the os shat itself after running 24/7 for a card that was designed as simple flash memory. after upgrading to a high endurance card, and finally an emmc module, it was back and stronger than ever. then i noticed i had a lot of cpu and ram overhead and thought - what else can i do with this?

turns out, a nextcloud server! i've ran 2 separate google drives for a while now, and i'm sorry but 15gb really isn't enough overhead. luckily, i'd just been gifted a samsung evo 870 1tb ssd, so i spent another day or two wrapping my head around nginx, tailscaled, and some other essential secure networking and got that online. instead of paying 15aud a month for 2tb, i pay 15qaud per *year* in energy costs for 1tb. very cool. very functional. so the question becomes this: how far can this whole self-hosting thing go? what if i could create a homelab that replaces all my paid subscriptions, let's me solve many of the minor frustrations other people have around their tech, and generally let me test linux to it's fullest? of course, for that i'd need a nuc perhaps, a micro pc, a das or nas, it'd be an investment, but all in due time!

you don't even use the server that much, why are you already considering expanding it?

psychotic setup

i recently decided on a whim to get actual peripherals for my laptop. i've always been slightly adverse to investing in full desktop. laptop's are cute! you can take them anywhere! very handy for audio work where i only need to worry about setting up one machine rather than saying "ah fuck that xyz is on my battlestation at home." regretfully though, laptops have a tendency to make you functionally give up on peripherals; for most people a laptop is a device to watch young sheldon on in bed, play deltarune fanmods and as a conversation starter as you gesture to it as 'that old thing' and talk about how beat up it is (probably from the amount of young sheldon you've watched) . for me, it's always been my primary 'computer', but as a result i've inherited that heretical mindset i suppose. so for a solid 10 years now i've been working with a logitech m171 mouse. hardly a bad mouse i should say. it's what i used for uni, it was comfortable, it was how i was speedrunning getting over it with bennet foddy for christ sake, but i happen to live a street over from my city's go-to computer place and thought, if i spend so much time on my rgb light up nitrogen cooled 8 yottabyte ram machine, i might as well make it comfortable. so, after extensive research into what makes a good mouse, i came to the conclusion it's all bullshit, and got a lofree touch and figured whatever it was, it was better than what i had. and honestly? yeah! so good, i ended up investing in the lofreee block keyboard (after consulting the mistress of the keys hollis and also buying some silent tactiles) and the lofree desk mat. all investments fi made after trying to maximize my desk space with a laptop stand and quickly figuring out it is a godawful way to type. i like the vibe. it's comfortable. responsive/ artistically cohesive. aesthetic (noun)

i thought you said you were a minimalist, a pragmatic. why are you spending so much on a setup? this isn't like you.

breaking point

i reached a point where i gave out. despite what many people believe, and what multiple pages on the internet arguably testify - i don't consider myself truly emotionally 'thrown off' by a lot. life is a series of small leaks and cracks that appear in the hull, and you find increasingly comical ways to plug the holes. it's honestly great fun. i *verbally process things*, to me that's not the same as having genuinely constant crash outs. they're cut from a different cloth. but i finally had a point of exhaustion, which gave way to emotional exhaustion.

it took months for me to recover. i'm still not 'better'. i'm still figuring it out, and it doesn't help that life also happened to pick up around the same time. repairs are actually something i do for money now! which you'd think would stress me out more but at least allows my stress to be commodified, and i've learnt a lot in the few i've done. i had my birthday, so i got myself a 3d printer, which has opened up a new vector of projects, but they're the kind of projects that're manageable and don't require a whole lot of time beyond finding models for things when the need arises. i've made some monitor stands, some storage for components and some decorative bits and pieces. i saw some people. i had a few medical appointments, i moved, i moved work locations, life has chugged on as it always has, and despite how it may seem, i'm poising myself for a lot of positive changes in both myself and what's around me, it just so happens that these things are precipitated by periods of uncertainty, stress and a lot of dabbling. what a fucking scam.

so, no, i'm doing okay. maybe after this i'll post another blog or two to get you up to speed, but right now, i'm adjusting. eating is good, sleeping is good, coming to terms with how my adhd masked some of my autistic traits and manifested awful truly coping mechanisms was hard, but seeing the person i want to be and the person i am come closer together has been a lifesaver. seriously.
